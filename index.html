Step 01

Interest is the largest line item

On a $400,000 loan at 6.50% over 30 years, total interest reaches roughly $510,100 — about 56% of everything you repay. Seeing that share before you commit changes how you shop for a rate.

Step 02

Half a point rewrites the budget

Moving from 6.50% to 7.00% adds about $133 per month — roughly $48,000 over the full term. The same math works in reverse, which is exactly what a refinance review measures.

Open Rate Impact
Step 03

Time decides how much you keep

Early payments are almost all interest; late payments are almost all principal. Shortening the term or adding to each payment moves you down that curve faster and cuts the total cost.

Questions before the math

Mortgage calculation, answered plainly

A fixed-rate payment covers interest on the outstanding balance plus principal repayment. Property tax, insurance and any association dues are usually collected alongside it but are not part of the principal-and-interest figure the calculator returns.

On the illustrative $400,000 loan over 30 years, each 0.25% costs roughly $65 a month. Held for the full term, a single point of difference moves lifetime interest by tens of thousands, which is why the rate impact tool exists.
